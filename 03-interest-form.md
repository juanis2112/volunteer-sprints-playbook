# Interest form

The application form for prospective participants, used to match people to projects and to support selection.

**When:** Draft at T-12w, test at T-11w, open at T-10w, close at [Application deadline] (about T-6w). Keep it open at least four weeks.

**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer.

**Audience:** Prospective participants at [Company].

## Template

### Form header

Sustaining Open Source Series: [Start date] to [End date]

[Company], in collaboration with [Foundation] and [Partner companies], is running a 10-week program supporting [Project list]. Participants are matched to a primary and a secondary project, get mentorship from project maintainers and Company mentors, join a small mini-cohort with a lead who checks in weekly, and attend weekly office hours. Contribution includes code, documentation, testing, reviews, triage, and community participation.

[If your company has a volunteer-hour matching program: Volunteer hours are eligible for matching through [Matching program name].]

- Series dates: [Start date] to [End date]
- Office hours: [Office hours day and time, with UTC]
- Application deadline: [Application deadline]
- Decisions sent by: [Decision date]
- Questions: [Program organizer name], [Contact]. FAQ: [FAQ link]

This form takes about [N] minutes. Thoughtful, specific answers help us a lot.

### Section 1: About you

- Full name *
- Work email *
- Office location or country *
- Region / time zone * (multiple choice: [Region 1], [Region 2], [Region 3], Other). Used to plan office hours and any regional cohorts.
- Team or organization *
- Public GitHub username * (your github.com account, not an internal one. Don't have one? Create one for free at github.com/signup and enter it here.)
- [Optional, if your company uses an employee ID for tracking: Employee ID]

### Section 2: Your skills

- How would you rate your [Primary language] skills? * (1-10, Beginner to Expert)
- How comfortable are you with [Domain skill, e.g. "working with dataframes, arrays, and numerical computing"]? * (1-10, Beginner to Expert)

### Section 3: The projects

For each project in [Project list]:

- Familiarity: how well do you know this project? * (1 = never used it, 5 = I've contributed before)
- Excitement: how excited are you to contribute here? * (1 = curious to learn more, 5 = can't wait to dig in)
- How have you used this project? (Optional short answer: day job, side projects, coursework, not yet.) Specific examples help.

### Section 4: Rank your projects

- Rank the projects in order of preference. * (Grid: one row per project, one column per rank. One choice per row and per column.)
- Your top choice is considered for your primary project and your second choice for your secondary project. We will do our best to honor your ranking, but placement also depends on mentor capacity.

### Section 5: Your open source story

- Have you contributed to open source before? * (Yes / No)
- If yes: tell us about it, with links if you have them (public open source only). Include any contributions to the projects above.
- If no: what has held you back, and what would help?
- What's pulling you toward these projects? Is there something specific you want to fix, improve, document, or learn? *
- Pick a real open issue (or other contribution) in your top-choice project and tell us, in a few sentences, how you'd approach it. Link the issue. There's no wrong answer; we want to see how you think. *
- Besides code, which kinds of community participation interest you? (Checkboxes: Joining project chat and answering questions / Attending community or working group meetings / Issue triage and reproducing bugs / Reviewing pull requests / Documentation and tutorials / Testing / Not sure yet, I'd like to explore)

### Section 6: Series background

- Have you participated in a previous Sustaining Open Source Series? * (No / Yes, as a participant / Yes, as a mentor). If yes, which one?
- Did you attend the Setup Sprint (a session before applications opened where people tested their development environments)? * (Yes / No / I didn't know about it)
- Are you part of an employee community, such as an employee resource group, guild, or community of practice? (Optional: Yes, which one / No)

### Section 7: Can you commit?

- Please check all that apply: *
  - I can carve out about [N] hours per week for the 10-week series
  - I can attend at least [N] office hours sessions ([Office hours day and time, with UTC])
  - I can check in with my mini-cohort lead once a week
- [If your company requires internal approval to contribute: I understand I will need to complete [Approval process], including manager approval, if selected. *]
- What do you hope to walk away with from this experience?
- Anything else we should know about you?

### Confirmation screen

Thank you for applying to the Sustaining Open Source Series! We'll send decisions by [Decision date]. In the meantime, join us at the info session on [Info session date and time] ([Zoom link]), and take a look at [Project list] and their contributing guides.

## How to use

- Update the header, project list, dates, office hours time, and deadline; check the confirmation message matches.
- Submit a test response yourself and confirm the linked response sheet captures every field. Delete the test row.
- Keep the ranking grid strict (one choice per row and column) so primary and secondary placements are unambiguous.
- Restrict response-sheet access to the selection team; responses contain personal data.
- Delete any conditional lines that don't apply at your company, and adjust the skills questions to the projects' languages.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public core + company add-on. Questions are shared so cross-company selection is comparable; approval, matching, and ID fields are company-specific.
- **Company-specific parts:** matching program line, approval acknowledgment, employee ID field, region options, employee community examples.
- **Derived from:** Juanita's playbook (Stage 2, Task 1, item 1: interest form template); NumFOCUS Series application form (column headers of the response sheet only: location, team, public GitHub, skills, familiarity, excitement, usage, ranking, open source history, prior series, Setup Sprint, time and office hours commitment, hopes, guild and employee community membership).
