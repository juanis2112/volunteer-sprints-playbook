# Maintainer kickoff agenda

A short agenda for the first meeting with project maintainers, to explain the series, hear what projects want, and agree next steps.

**When:** T-20w (after the foundation has agreed to explore).
**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer, co-facilitated with the co-organizer company if any
**Audience:** Maintainers and foundation staff from each candidate project

## Template

**Sustaining Open Source Series: project kickoff**
[Date], [Time, with UTC and the main time zones of attendees], 45 to 60 min, [Zoom link]

**Goals**
- Give projects a clear picture of how the series works.
- Hear what each project wants and what would make them hesitate.
- Leave knowing which projects are in, and the preferred timing window.

**1. Welcome and lightning intros (5 to 8 min)**
- Name, project, role, one sentence on what interests you.
- Frame: informal, questions welcome, no pressure.

**2. What the series is (7 to 10 min)**
- The problem: companies depend on open source, maintainer capacity is the bottleneck.
- The model: a 10-week cohort, company mentors plus project mentors, weekly office hours, anchored by [Foundation]. [If applicable: volunteer hours matched to donations.]
- What it is not: a hackathon, or surprise pull requests. Maintainers set the priorities.
- [If applicable: results from a prior run, with period and source.]
- [If applicable: what is new this time, for example a multi-project or multi-company cohort.]

**3. What do projects want? (10 min, open conversation)**
- What are your goals for the series?
- Which contributions help most: code, docs, tests, triage, reviews, community participation?
- What sits in your backlog that you wish you had more hands for?
- How do you onboard new contributors today? We want to build on it.
- How is AI-generated work affecting your review queue, and how can the series avoid adding to it?

**4. What we ask of projects (8 to 10 min)**
- Before: onboarding docs and series-ready issues or contribution areas (the project profile).
- During: [N] maintainers across weekly office hours (about 1 hour a week), and reviews of work that mentors have already helped shape.
- Principle: share responsibility without taking control. You set the direction; we staff the work.

**5. Timing (5 to 8 min)**
- Option A: [Start date option A].
- Option B: [Start date option B].
- Does avoiding [Holiday period] matter for your maintainer availability?

**6. Next steps (4 to 5 min)**
- Project profile request goes out after this call, due [Confirm-by date].
- Confirm participation and timing by [Confirm-by date].
- How we will stay in touch: [Hub channel or mailing list].

**Facilitator notes**
- Keep it conversational. Have one "how it works" slide ready but do not lean on it.
- Watch for three concerns and address them directly: review burden (pre-alignment and mentor support, no surprise pull requests), contributor quality (structured preparation before anyone touches the codebase), and control (the project leads; the foundation is the neutral anchor).
- If projects start problem-solving with each other, let it run.
- If a project seems unsure, do not push. Offer a follow-up conversation.

## How to use

- Fill in time zones for every region attending.
- Send the agenda in the invite, and the project profile request (04-project-profile-request.md) right after.
- Take notes on each project's goals, concerns, and preferred timing; these drive the profile follow-ups.
- Expect some attrition between kickoff and launch. Do not announce the project list publicly until profiles are in.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public. The flow works for any company or foundation.
- **Company-specific parts:** facilitators, prior-run results, matching language.
- **Derived from:** "NumFOCUS Summer Sustainability Series: Project Planning Kickoff" agenda; "NumFocus Summer Series Project Info Session" agenda.
