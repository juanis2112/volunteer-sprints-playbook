## The model in one page

What the series is:

- A fixed-length cohort (10 weeks) where employees contribute to an open source project with sanctioned time, curated issues, and mentors from both the company and the open source project.
- Ideally, philanthropy matches the volunteer hours with donations to the foundation. That match is the mechanism that makes it volunteering.

The five design pillars:

- Fixed 10 weeks.
- Issues curated before day one.
- One weekly office hour.
- Company mentors and project maintainers.
- Sanctioned time.

What a company must bring:

- A Team Lead.
- Company mentors at about 1 per 10 participants.
- Manager-approved time of 2 to 4 hours a week.
- Internal comms.

What an open source project must bring:

- 2 or more maintainers willing to mentor.
- A curated list of series-ready issues.
- Reviewer bandwidth.
- A comms channel.
- A published AI policy.
- A definition of success. Ralf Gommers, 2026: "If 1 contributor per project stays around long-term, that'd be success."

The lesson every run repeats:

- Issue discovery is the bottleneck, not motivation.
- Over-curate issues, and name an owner who refreshes the list every week.

## Roles

Source: [InTech] NumFOCUS Series Roles & Expectations, the canonical roles doc.

* **Program Owner (OSPO):** timeline, foundation and partner relationships, agreement, selection, comms, BOB requests, weekly notes and announcements, reporting.
* **Team Lead (at least 1 per company):** acts as program manager for their company. About 50 hours total, across about 6 weeks before, 10 during, and 1 to 2 after. Administers SDSK tickets (manager approvals, check-ins, logged hours).
* **Mini-cohort lead:** a named person who checks in 1:1 with a small group every week. Goal: catch people who are quietly stuck before they drop off.
* **Company Mentor (at least 2 per company):** first line of support. At least 2 office hours, 2 to 4 hrs/week, environment setup, Slack.
* **Project Mentor (open source project maintainers):** at least 3 office hours, about 2 hrs/week. Curates issues, reviews PRs, leads project breakouts.
* **Corporate Philanthropy:** signs the agreement, builds the BOB events, awards matched hours (capped at 50 per person).
* **Participant:** 2 to 4 hrs/week for 10 weeks, files SDSK ADD 43571, logs hours, attends office hours, answers surveys.


Each step lists **When / Owner / Do / Artifacts / Lessons**. Steps 1 to 13 follow the Framework. Steps 3a, 5a, and 5b fill gaps the Framework does not cover.

## Phase 1: Strategic alignment and setup (T-24w to T-12w)

### Step 1: Foundation partnership and agreement

- **When:**
  - First contact T-24w.
  - Start the agreement by T-16w. Allow up to 2 months for signature.
- **Requirement:** a signed agreement with the foundation is required for every run.
- **Owner:** Program Owner, with Philanthropy, who signs.
- **Do:**
  - Pitch the model to the foundation. The OTel pitch to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF) is the reference.
  - Execute a volunteer-services letter agreement. The pattern (NumFOCUS 2023, CNCF 2026):
    - Schedule A states the term and the program ("new contributor friendly" issues).
    - Volunteers commit up to 2 hrs/week.
    - 2 foundation mentors plus 2 Bloomberg mentors.
    - Use of Bloomberg's name needs written approval.
  - Confirm the foundation is the charity of record on BOB.
  - For multi-company runs, also put NDAs in place with industry partners.
- **Artifacts:**
  - Executed NumFOCUS 2023 letter ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=13Of_ZkITU0JfRFwPYIe-iCC-Be-crNFz)).
  - CNCF / OpenTelemetry Terms ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=11WZEGGLdvVvZTmsBjr5MttyLCuzaTGujRRviCTtf2fo)).
  - Pitch email ([Series] CNCF Mentorship, [Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1iOhoTk6k8luC1HPTwUwAsuBdYVFIZDPEWfz4EYt-yds)).
- **Lessons:**
  - The agreements carry no fee. Money flows only through the hours match, so be precise about that in pitches.
  - Agreements also protect how each party's name is used in comms.

### Step 2: Upstream project readiness (in parallel)

- **When:** T-20w to T-12w. Project profiles are due T-14w.
- **Owner:** Program Owner, plus Project Mentors.
- **Do:**
  - Kickoff with maintainers. Ask each project for:
    - Overview and onboarding instructions.
    - 15 or more series-ready issues, each sized to merge in about a week.
    - Reviewer bandwidth and a wish list.
    - AI policy, comms channel, and definition of success.
  - Agree on a series label, or decide not to use one. NumPy used a series-only label so issues were not "sniped." SciPy declined public labels because they get "polluted." OTel used mentorship:bloomberg.
  - Agree the mentee journey: 1 to 2 curated issues first, then open up to wider repos after 1 to 2 merged PRs. Severin's OTel model is the reference.
- **Artifacts:**
  - [Projects] Overview & Mentor List ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=10EAVr3g30yHyy-Y17Ua6HmjUxNzdEkJtpSY_NWmpwSs)).
  - [Internal] Project Info ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=12arxVTmnVLo_q00UosyXLRe5ekqxqkowCQGR9FDoGJY)).
  - Issue Curation & Mentee Journey ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=13qCczE2NrwGTZ3VypFKiR7c32Cgpb-C6NuBKQteiKTY)).
- **Lessons:**
  - Expect project attrition. 8 projects were confirmed on 5/20/2026 and 5 went ahead.
  - Language and mentor coverage gaps hurt. OTel had no Python or C++ mentor, and those PRs were harder to get reviewed.
  - "Maintainers will treat the mentees not different to any other contributor." Set that expectation with participants.

### Step 3: Stakeholder alignment (in parallel)

- **When:** T-20w to T-10w.
- **Owner:** Program Owner.
- **Do:**
  - Cross-org kickoff: success metrics, cohort size, mentor ratio, dates, time slot.
  - Philanthropy intro, plus partner philanthropy for multi-company runs.
  - Avoid August and holidays.
  - Check partner conflicts.
  - Pick the weekly slot for maximum time-zone coverage. 11am ET / 15:00 UTC worked for New York, London, and the US West Coast. 4 to 6pm ET locked out London in 2023.
- **Artifacts:**
  - [Notes] Bloomberg <> NVIDIA Corporate Philanthropy Intro ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1jOeZJaUEthGZgay03JCL8FBU_EXIzCQvqV9ZXtwcHm0)).
  - [Running Notes] Numfocus Ecosystem Series ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1wUMHNuav-IJZ7v3KcvWJnGGTvY1NJmBA7dgOeVOaSAw)).

### Step 3a: Partner company recruitment (multi-company runs only)

- **When:** T-20w to T-14w.
- **Owner:** Program Owner, plus co-organizer company.
- **Do:**
  - Send co-signed outreach to candidate companies.
  - Share the Company Overview.
  - Confirm each company's Team Lead, mentors, and (if applicable) philanthropy matching program.
  - Hand each company the [Partner Guide](PARTNER-GUIDE.md).
- **Artifacts:**
  - [Comms] Industry Outreach ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1xJRzRxGi2umhuK7DvR1HWXSyPc_ReyLRqufr1F4algo)).

## Phase 2: Series infrastructure and mentorship (T-14w to T-4w)

### Step 4: Select the mentor core

- **When:** Recruit T-14w to T-8w. Mentor meet-and-greet about T-1w.
- **Owner:** Program Owner, plus Team Leads.
- **Do:**
  - Recruit company mentors at about 1:10. Series alumni make strong mentors.
  - Confirm open source project mentor coverage so every office hour week has 2 or more mentors. Aim for 3 to 4 project mentors in any given week.
  - Share Roles & Expectations.
  - Collect deep-dive topics and agree the sync cadence. Weekly before T0, every two weeks from about T+3w.
- **Role minimums:**
  - Company Mentor: at least 2 office hours, 2 to 4 hrs/week.
  - Project Mentor: at least 3 office hours, about 2 hrs/week.
  - Team Lead: about 50 hrs total, across about 6 weeks before, 10 during, and 1 to 2 after.
- **Artifacts:**
  - [InTech] NumFOCUS Series Roles & Expectations ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=12PtLnfKM0HjGfuX4irdRq_ORU9rH9hPlP3VJ33-bOqA)).

### Step 5: Communication channels

- **When:** Decide by T-10w, live by T-4w.
- **Owner:** Program Owner. Philanthropy for BOB.
- **Do:**
  - Pick ONE hub and publish an "Official Channels" doc before T0. Never rely on people finding channels themselves.
  - Program Owner or Team Lead must be in every private series channel, for legal and monitoring reasons.
  - Use a private announcements channel plus public per-project channels. One channel with hundreds of people "is impossible to follow."
  - Set up a shared inbox (os-mentorship@bloomberg.net), one recurring Zoom link with recording and AI notes, and a home-base docs page (on Tutti).
  - Use a Shared Drive folder for organizers. Individual sharing failed for partners.
- **Artifacts:**
  - Official Program Communication Channels (doc [Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1EX6GIMaCS5AZqNJ5IiQrp-5m-Db7kNXBnrjIbHXm2gg), sheet [Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1B3UdAGcKtvacacxKUmMx9-G1V-hAOySCbZwVzX0meyw)).
  - Collaboration Guidance (several near-identical copies exist; any of them works as the starting template, see [Resources](RESOURCES.md)).
- **Lessons:**
  - Channel sprawl was the biggest operational pain in 2026: Slack, IB, email, Discord, and four Slack workspaces.
  - Some participants (data department) could not get Slack. Discord images are blocked on the Bloomberg network.
  - Workspaces like NumPy's require a personal email. Decide the fallbacks before launch.

### Step 5a: Dev environment pre-work (Setup Sprint)

- **When:** T-13w to T-11w, before applications open. Standard for every run.
- **Owner:** Program Owner, plus Philanthropy (BOB events), plus an employee resource group (BProud in 2026).
- **Do:**
  - Run 1 to 2 in-person setup sprints with about 30 people in groups of 3 to 5. Each counts as a BOB event worth 4 hours.
  - Test every project's setup guide on Mac with bbVPN, Windows WSL, and Spaces/Codespaces.
  - Publish setup guides at a bburl.
  - Rules: personal GitHub account; bloomberg.net as a secondary email, never primary; sign CLAs with personal email.
- **Artifacts:**
  - [COMMS] Sustainability Sprint ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1DdeuUrr0medkfKeYKs60bQFJwiyhfguCcvmb6-lbGzw)).
  - [Comms] PRIDE Sprint ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1Xp8FUwr7DGrtBpyTxSGaVcPMSZlnSF-X2Ao2G3vf--4)).
  - NumFOCUS OSS Setup Guides ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1aOrHxnBT6-H4lEg0OIEAFs90sz7ooM-Sa8DZxDkGqzk)).
- **Lessons:**
  - Setup was the top friction in pandas 2023 and 2024.
  - Known blockers:
    - Internal npm mirror lag of about 2 weeks.
    - Proxy and SSL problems.
    - Pixi failing on some laptops.
    - The OpenBLAS error.
  - Codespaces is the reliable fallback.
  - Sprints reduced but did not remove kickoff errors. The setup guide had 26 open comment threads, so close those before T0.

### Step 5b: IPWG and contribution approvals

- **When:** Decide by T-8w.
- **Owner:** Program Owner.
- **Do:**
  - Decide how series contributions are pre-cleared so participants do not wait on a ticket mid-series.
  - Confirm the CLA path for each project.
  - Add any new CLA through the IPWG process early.

## Phase 3: Cohort recruitment and selection (T-9w to T-3w)

### Step 6: Interest campaign and selection

- **When:** Form live T-8w. Info session T-6w. Form closes T-6w. Scoring T-6w to T-5w. Decisions T-5w.
- **Owner:** Program Owner, plus selection committee (5 to 7 reviewers).
- **Do:**
  - Build an outreach tracker (channel, driver, status). Use chats, newsletters (Engineering, ONBB, BWIT), BLST, the DevX banner, and employee resource groups.
  - Update the Tutti FAQ and timeline.
  - Run a 60-minute info session as a BOB event: 30 minutes of intros, then an AMA. Record it.
  - Intake form must cover:
    - Skills (1-10 per language or tool).
    - Prior open source experience.
    - Project ranking.
    - 2 to 4 hrs/week commitment.
    - Office hours availability.
    - Motivations, prior series, Guild and InTech membership.
    - GitHub handle.
  - Scoring (current guide):
    - Each reviewer scores 1-10 holistically in their own tab.
    - Factors: real usage, open source excitement or experience, application effort, community engagement.
    - Bands: 9-10 strong on all; 6-8 solid on 2-3; 3-5 thin; 1-2 does not meet the bar.
    - Notes are required on 1-2 and 9-10.
    - Flag personal connections.
    - Do not score prior participation up or down; the Program Owner brings a GitHub check on past participants instead.
    - Usage alone never earns 7+. It needs a specific in-scope target.
  - Consolidate into total, average, and variance. Settle the gray zone in a work session.
  - Cap popular projects' primary slots by reviewer capacity, and protect small projects.
- **Artifacts:**
  - [Advertisement] doc ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1XkBwboURZtdjv5d2k6zGiiHoaEtk-RRpJskhjErYVzg)).
  - Tutti Documentation ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1rj5pHWiJWdoBwFFxcpwPA3gIFfuvpWAPePveF3hNJis)).
  - Intake form ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1FpNvhj1FXS8WraGKk4EEY_DXM-MYDUIFS3h0zbl_xEE)).
  - Scoring Guide ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1t5XUFxDmLlJgECNMVGoAH1LoFgMdQzQuqkvVJVHHcqA)).
  - [Responses] sheet ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1pvAvNXZ7mYPvC66NHyAfNimZV2l5_9zxsPl0nj2Zq6w)).
  - Previous Participant Activity ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1f-fM0jrV5VvEJRKCn1s3A464nDxJ_BnzRIpfeqCsWg4)).
  - OTel Cohort Response analysis ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1BNlYvvlS2etNyrJQwhPKMA1o65B6sjvuJx_PQoTRYX0)) as the model for persona analysis.
- **Lessons:**
  - Expect demand of 3 to 6 times the slots:
    - OTel: 135 applied for about 45.
    - NumFOCUS: about 300 for about 45.
  - A persona analysis (Bridge-Builders, First-Timers, Targeted Contributors) shaped the OTel curriculum. Share an anonymized version with maintainers.
- **APAC cohort (standard):**
  - Run an APAC sub-cohort every time, recruited with the APAC community.
  - Filter APAC applicants into a separate sheet and have APAC mentors and regional reviewers score them.
  - Hold an APAC regional kickoff.

### Step 7: Cohort onboarding

- **When:** Emails T-5w. Confirmed by T-4w. Waitlist pulls until T-2w, then close.
- **Owner:** Program Owner, plus Team Leads.
- **Do:**
  - Send personalized acceptances by mail merge, with a confirm-by date.
  - The Program Owner emails every accepted participant's manager.
  - Waitlist and not-selected emails route people to OSS Dollars For Your Hours (SDSK 40698), naming the correct foundation.
  - Participants file the series SDSK ticket, SDSK ADD 43571, by T-1w. ("#SOSS" in older templates means 43571.)
  - Collect GitHub handles, and confirm Slack and channel joins.
  - Assign every participant a mini-cohort lead (standard from 2026). Goal: "catch people who are quietly stuck before they (silently) drop off." Group by project or region; keep groups small enough for weekly 1:1 check-ins (London's 16 was the upper end).
  - Send pre-work 1 week before T0: environment set up, contributing guides read, intro posted.
- **Artifacts:**
  - Cohort Selection Emails ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1sy1T_5qrSEDVy4lL6aNmqR9FAwK6lUy2zQA1VTTWPjg)).
  - OTel version ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1jrDT1k6tG6A7zh0hZJuV4wrOvReOwX1H7JFjD1Ywxbs)).
  - Cohort Status Tracker ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1oQtEfO-3mfGAplK2Tx7Mw5Wo4ey_9iTLAfOLyAu2bAs)).
- **Lessons:**
  - About 10% decline after acceptance. People added late need catch-up support.
  - Setting up Slack channels can be hard and manual.

## Phase 4: The series in action (T0 to T+9w)

### Step 8: Office hours rhythm

See [OFFICE-HOURS.md](OFFICE-HOURS.md) for the session kit. Summary:

- Fixed weekly hour.
- Session shape: wins, then a 20-minute deep dive, then 30 to 40 minutes of breakouts, then wrap-up.
- Every week: agenda, poll, AI notes reviewed against the recording, and a "notes live" post.

### Step 9: Async support and engagement

- **Owner:** Team Leads, mini-cohort leads, mentors.
- **Do:**
  - Team Leads monitor channels daily.
  - Project mentors answer on Slack and review on GitHub.
  - At T+4w, run an engagement triage: SDSK hours plus GitHub activity plus attendance. In 2026, 33 of 56 showed no activity at that point.
  - Follow up 1:1 through mini-cohort leads. "Make it a check-in, not a chase." Individual messages outperform group pings.
- **Lessons:**
  - AI-generated PRs without disclosure will happen. Have the reminder message ready, and confirm each project's AI policy, which overrides series guidance.
  - Remove leaderboards. They "could be easily gamed." Use a non-ranked list kept confidential.

### Step 10: Tracking and metrics

See [METRICS.md](METRICS.md).

## Phase 5: Conclusion and recognition (T+9w to T+14w)

### Step 11: Feedback and evaluation

- Midpoint check-in at about T+4w to T+5w. Present the results the following week, labeled self-reported and internal.
- End-of-series survey opens the week after the final session and stays open for 2 to 3 weeks.
- Mentor and organizer retro at about T+4w after close.
- Write the post-mortem with stakeholders.

### Step 12: Celebration session

- Final session: participant stories (mentors recruit one featured participant each, 5 minutes), mentor reflections, what's next.
- Optional in-person celebration event (OTel: AMNH Planetarium).

### Step 13: Recognition and continuity

- OSPO sends manager recognition notes for year-end evaluations, one per team lead and mentor, in the last few weeks of the series (2026: Week 7 of 10).
  - Template: [Manager Recognition Email Template](https://docs.google.com/document/d/155iJptv9vszIrGLh3X6MB-vCFjQcUpde/edit). Covers when to send, a pre-send checklist, Template A (manager knows the series), Template B (first contact), and role prompts.
  - Earlier version: 2024 EVAL Notes ([Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1PzPbt2ulO2sFzkYDMWnzM5sYc3nI9cz2xXj7-yzk3ys)).
- Continuation routes:
  - SDSK 40698 (DFYH, personal time).
  - SDSK 39037 (work contributions).
  - Project community meetings.
  - Guild or working groups.
