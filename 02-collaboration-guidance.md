Participant-facing guidance on display names, the hub, introductions, communication norms, and AI use.

## Template

### Sustaining Open Source Series: collaboration guidance

This series brings together participants, mentors, and maintainers from [N] companies ([Partner companies]) and [N] projects ([Project list]). With so many people collaborating at once, a name alone does not tell us who you are, which company you are with, or which project you are working on. A few small setup steps make a big difference.

**Zoom setup**

Zoom is where we meet live. Weekly office hours ([Office hours day and time, with UTC]) include walkthroughs (code tours, git workflow, debugging, testing), breakout rooms by topic (finding a first issue, reviews, group projects), and time to work alongside others. The link is posted in [Hub channel] before each session.

Set your display name to: First Last (Company, Project). Example: Jane Doe ([Company], [Project]).

1. Sign in to Zoom (web or app).
2. Open Profile.
3. Click Edit next to your name.
4. Enter "First Last (Company, Project)" as your display name.
5. Save.

**Hub setup ([Hub platform])**

[Hub channel] in the [Hub workspace] workspace is home base between office hours: announcements, office hours agendas and links, polls, notes, and shared resources.

You will also work closely with your project mentors in your project's own space ([Project channel]). That is where the most hands-on collaboration happens, in the open.

1. Join the [Hub workspace] workspace using your personal email address [or the address your company's open source guidance requires].
2. Ask your Team Lead to add you to the private series channel.
3. Complete your profile:
   - Full name.
   - Display name: First Last (Company, Project).
   - Profile photo.
   - Title and pronouns, if you like.
   - Your code hosting profile URL (for example GitHub) in the About section.

If you cannot access [Hub platform], tell your Team Lead right away. See the Official Channels doc for the fallback.

**Introduce yourself**

Post a short intro in [Hub channel]:
- Your name, company, and project.
- Your role or what draws you to the project.
- What you hope to get out of the series, and any part of the project you want to learn.
- A fun fact, if you like.

Keep it to one message.

Example:

```text
Hi everyone, I'm [Participant first name] from [Company], working with [Project]. I'm a data engineer and this is my first real open source contribution. I'd love to dig into the I/O layer and learn how reviews work here. Fun fact: I'm learning the ukulele.
```

**Communication norms**
- Ask questions in channels, not DMs, so others can learn from the answer.
- Search the channel and project docs first, then ask. Say what you tried.
- Share blockers early. Being stuck for a week helps nobody.
- Celebrate wins, and support other contributors. Answering questions counts as contribution too.
- Maintainers are volunteers and treat series participants like any other contributor. Be patient and respectful.
- Follow each project's code of conduct and channel conventions.

**AI usage guidance**

AI coding tools can help you learn a new codebase and move faster. Open source contribution also comes with expectations from maintainers, from each project's community, and from your employer about how AI is used. This series is about becoming a trusted, effective contributor, so these principles are the baseline. We will go deeper in office hours.

1. **Follow each project's AI policy first.** Projects set their own rules and they differ. When a project's policy and this guidance conflict, the project's policy wins.
2. **Be transparent.** Disclose AI use in your contribution, for example "I used an AI tool to help with X" in the pull request description. When in doubt, disclose.
3. **Take responsibility.** You own what you submit. Verify correctness, security, and licensing before you open a pull request.
4. **Demonstrate understanding.** You must be able to explain every change. If you cannot walk through it, it is not ready for review.
5. **Respect maintainer time.** AI lowers the cost of creating contributions, not reviewing them. Keep changes small and tested. Keep descriptions concise.
6. **Engage authentically.** Write your own replies to reviewers. They expect to talk with the human author, and that conversation is how you learn and build relationships.
