# Roles and expectations

Defines every role in the series, with responsibilities, minimums, and time commitments, so all companies and projects share one understanding.

**When:** Share with mentors and Team Leads at T-14w; with participants at acceptance (T-5w).
**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer
**Audience:** Team Leads, company mentors, project mentors, mini-cohort leads, participants

## Template

### Sustaining Open Source Series: roles and expectations

This document describes what each role does across [Partner companies] and [Project list], so everyone knows what to expect from each other.

**Program organizer**

The program organizer owns the series end to end.
- Timeline, foundation and partner relationships, and any agreements.
- Selection process, comms, and the weekly notes and announcements.
- Shared infrastructure: office hours, channels, the Official Channels doc.
- Reporting, with period, population, and source on every number.

**Team Lead (at least 1 per company)**

Team Leads are the program managers for their company's side of the series: the glue between goals and day-to-day execution. They support mentors and help participants engage, learn, and grow.

Before the series (about 6 weeks):
- Confirm dates; prepare announcements and an FAQ.
- Run internal announcements, sign-up, and info sessions.
- Handle participant selection, dev environment materials, and office hours scheduling.

During the series (10 weeks):
- Take part in and help facilitate weekly office hours.
- Watch channels and answer operational questions.
- Track progress, run activity reports, and help draft the mid-series survey.
- Write up notes and share them in the channels.
- Work with mentors on themed sessions with project maintainers.
- [If your company requires internal approval or hour tracking: Keep approvals and logged hours current through [Approval process].]

After the series (1 to 2 weeks):
- Send the post-series survey and gather results.
- Lead or join the retro with key stakeholders.

Time: about 2 to 4 hours a week across pre, during, and post, about 50 hours in total. Expect heavier weeks before kickoff.

**Company mentor (about 1 per 10 participants, at least 2 per company)**

Company mentors are employees of a participating company with open source experience. They are the first line of support.
- Guide participants in contributing to their assigned project.
- Attend at least 2 weekly office hours.
- Answer questions in the series channels.
- Help their company's participants set up dev environments.
- Give technical feedback as work progresses.
- Work closely with Team Leads and project mentors.

Time: at least about 2 to 4 hours a week.

**Project mentor (open source maintainers and regular contributors)**

Project mentors provide expert guidance on their project.
- Attend at least 3 office hours across the series.
- Offer insight into the codebase, contribution process, and community norms.
- Identify series-ready issues and larger team projects; coordinate with other maintainers on labels.
- Lead project breakouts when relevant.
- Review contributions as they come in.

Participants will gravitate toward mentors who match their interests, and that is fine. Mentors support the group as a whole, so individuals can step away when out of office as long as the group stays covered. Aim for 3 to 4 project mentors available in any given week.

Time: at least about 2 hours a week.

**Mini-cohort lead**

A named person (often a Team Lead, company mentor, or series alumnus) responsible for a small group, grouped by project or region.
- Check in 1:1 with each person every week. Make it a check-in, not a chase.
- Catch people who are quietly stuck before they drop off.
- Escalate blockers to mentors or the Team Lead.
- Keep groups small enough for weekly 1:1s.

Time: about [N] hours a week, depending on group size.

**Participant**

Participants are employees of a partner company selected to contribute to one or more projects.
- Get set up: complete the dev environment setup for your project(s) using the setup guides.
- Show up: attend weekly office hours when you can, and join the series and project channels.
- Contribute: code, docs, tests, triage, reviews, answering questions, and joining community meetings all count.
- Follow project norms: read each project's contributing guide and AI policy before contributing, and follow the series collaboration guidance.
- Ask and share: raise blockers early and celebrate wins with the group.
- Give feedback: complete the mid-series and post-series surveys.
- [If your company tracks volunteer hours: Log your time through [Hour-tracking process] and keep it updated.]

Time: about 2 to 4 hours a week for 10 weeks, including office hours.

**Shared responsibilities for all mentors**
- Attend and actively support weekly office hours.
- Answer questions in the channels.
- Take part in the retro.

## How to use

- Fill in the project list, companies, and any company-specific tracking lines before sharing.
- Ask every mentor to confirm the minimums in writing (a reply or form is enough).
- Check coverage: every office hours week should have at least 2 mentors, ideally 3 to 4 project mentors available.
- Share the participant section in acceptance emails, not only in a separate doc.
