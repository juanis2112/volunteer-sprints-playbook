# Company overview (one-pager)

A one-page summary for prospective partner companies: what the series is, what joining involves, roles, and what companies get.

**When:** T-20w, attached to the partner company outreach email.
**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer
**Audience:** OSPO leads, engineering leaders, and philanthropy or community contacts at prospective partner companies

## Template

### Sustaining Open Source Series with [Foundation]: overview for company partners

**What is the series?**

The Sustaining Open Source Series is a structured 10-week cohort program co-organized by [Company] and [Partner companies] in partnership with [Foundation]. Engineers from participating companies contribute to open source projects with guidance from project maintainers, starting from contribution pathways the projects define.

This is not a hackathon. It is a sustained, maintainer-first program. Maintainers set priorities, and the program is designed to reduce their load, not add to it.

This run includes [N] projects: [Project list].

**What participants do**

- Attend weekly office hours with maintainers and mentors (1 hour a week, [Office hours day and time, with UTC]).
- Work asynchronously with support in the series hub and project channels.
- Contribute in the ways each project needs: code, docs, tests, issue triage, reviews, answering questions, and joining community meetings.
- Get a named mini-cohort lead who checks in with them each week.

**What companies commit**

- Participant time: about 2 to 4 hours a week for 10 weeks. [If your company treats this as personal volunteer time, say so here.]
- A Team Lead: runs internal announcements, intake, selection, and participant support for your company. About 50 hours across the full series.
- Company mentors: about 1 per 10 participants (see below).
- Your own internal process for approvals and hour tracking.
- [If your company has a volunteer-hour matching program: Matching of volunteer hours through [Matching program name].]
- [If required: An agreement or NDA between partner companies. [Company] can share the structure.]

**The company mentor role**

Company mentors are engineers with open source experience who support their company's participants. This is the most important commitment beyond participation itself. Mentors:

- Attend at least 2 weekly office hours.
- Help participants set up dev environments and choose good first contributions.
- Watch the series channels and answer questions.
- Give technical feedback on work in progress.

Expected time: about 2 to 4 hours a week. Mentors from all partner companies work as one team, so nobody is on their own.

**What the organizers provide**

- The master schedule, weekly office hours, and run of show.
- Shared channels and the Official Channels doc.
- Coordination with the projects and the foundation.
- A cross-company sync every two weeks once the series is running.

**What companies get**

- Ecosystem investment with visible results: contributions to projects your business depends on, reported with period and source. [If you have verified prior-run results: [N] participants, [N] hours, [N] contributions in [Period], source: [Source].]
- Talent development: engineers learn to navigate unfamiliar codebases, work with maintainers, and contribute upstream.
- Lasting relationships: the program is built to turn first-time contributors into people who keep showing up in these communities.
- Co-organizer recognition alongside the other partners [and through [Foundation]'s channels, subject to each company's name-use approval].

**Timeline**

- Partner confirmation: [Confirm-by date]
- Setup Sprint (testing dev environments): [Date]
- Applications open and close: [Date] to [Application deadline]
- Series runs: [Start date] to [End date]

[Tip: avoid major holiday periods. Starting right after a common holiday month makes for a stronger cohort.]

**Questions?**

Contact [Program organizer name] ([Company]) or [Co-organizer name] ([Partner company]).

## How to use

- Keep it to one page. Cut sections before you add them.
- Only show results you can verify, labeled with period, population, and source. If you have none yet, delete the bracketed line rather than estimating.
- Do not state that partners match hours unless each has confirmed it. Matching is optional.
- Use bulleted lists, not tables, so it survives copy and paste into email.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public core + company add-on. Structure, roles, and commitments are shared; results, timeline, and agreement language are per run.
- **Company-specific parts:** organizer names, prior results, matching, agreement or NDA requirements, timeline.
