# Manager Recognition Email

A personal note to the manager of each mentor and Team Lead, recognizing their volunteer leadership as input for performance reviews.

**When:** T+6w to T+8w (the last few weeks of the series, before year-end reviews are written).

**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer.

**Audience:** The manager of one mentor or Team Lead. One email per person; if someone has two managers, address both.

## Template

### Template A: the manager knows the series

```text
To: [Manager name]
Subject: [Person full name]'s impact on the Sustaining Open Source Series

Hi [Manager first name],

As we head into the final weeks of the Sustaining Open Source Series, our partnership with [Partner companies] and [Foundation] across [Project list], I wanted to share how much [Person full name] has contributed as a [Team Lead / mentor / Team Lead and mentor], as input for [their] review.

[2 to 3 sentences on what they did, using the role prompts below.] [A specific moment: a session they led, a PR they reviewed, a participant they unblocked.]

The impact is real. From about [N] applications for [N] places, the cohort has made [N] contributions, including [N] pull requests accepted by project maintainers, and [N] people are showing up week after week (as of [Date]; source: [Activity report]).

This is volunteer work on top of [their] regular role, and it matters. When people like [First name] lead, our people grow, our company's influence in the open source projects we depend on grows, and we do real good in the world.

[If your company has a volunteer-hour matching program: [First name] will be credited with volunteer hours through [Matching program name], but numbers don't capture it.] Thank you for supporting [their] involvement!

Best,
[Program organizer name]
[Program organizer team]
```

### Template B: first contact with the manager

```text
To: [Manager name]
Subject: [Person full name]'s volunteer leadership in our open source mentorship program

Hi [Manager first name],

I'm [Program organizer name] from [Program organizer team]. I don't think we've met, so I wanted to introduce myself and share how much [Person full name] has contributed as a volunteer [mentor / Team Lead] this [season], as input for [their] review.

The Sustaining Open Source Series is a 10-week volunteer program where [Company] engineers contribute to widely used open source projects ([Project list]), mentored by the projects' own maintainers, in partnership with [Partner companies] and [Foundation]. [Optional: one line on what was new this run, e.g. a first regional cohort.]

[2 to 3 sentences on what they did, with key phrases in bold if your email client supports it.] [A specific moment.]

So far, the cohort has made [N] contributions, including [N] pull requests accepted by project maintainers (as of [Date]; source: [Activity report]). When people like [First name] lead, our people grow, our company's influence in the open source projects we depend on grows, and we do real good in the world.

[If your company has a volunteer-hour matching program: [First name] will be credited with volunteer hours through [Matching program name], but numbers don't capture it.] Thank you for supporting [their] involvement!

Best,
[Program organizer name]
[Program organizer team]
```

### Role prompts (pick the 2 or 3 that are true and specific)

- **Team Lead:** helped select the cohort from about [N] applicants; leads a mini-cohort; ran or co-led a Setup Sprint; keeps the program running week to week (channels, tracking, answering questions); owns part of how the series is measured; brought in participants through an employee resource group.
- **Company mentor:** leads a mini-cohort with weekly 1:1 check-ins; reviews participants' pull requests or helps them find a first contribution; led or co-created a session or breakout; covers a project few other mentors cover.
- **Maintainer-mentor:** maintains [Project] and brings project context and reviews few others can.
- **Former participant now mentoring:** shows new contributors what's possible; part of the participant-to-mentor pipeline.
- **Regional mentor:** built and drove the regional cohort; explained contribution and volunteering processes to their office.

## How to use

- Replace every bracket. Add one specific moment for each person; that's what makes the note worth reading.
- Refresh every number, give it an "as of" date and source, and use only figures you can verify. Prefer verified outcomes (accepted pull requests, attendance) over survey intent.
- Use the person's own pronouns. If you don't know them, write around it or use they/them.
- Use Template A when the manager knows your team or the series, Template B otherwise. Double-check the manager's name and the To line.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public core + company add-on. The structure is universal; review-cycle timing and the volunteer-hour line are company-specific.
- **Company-specific parts:** program organizer team name, matching program line, review-cycle timing, whether project maintainer-mentors (who may not be your employees) get a version addressed to someone else.
- **Derived from:** Manager Recognition Email Template (Eval Letters, Template A and B, role prompts).
