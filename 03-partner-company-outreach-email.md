# Partner company outreach email

Invites another company to join a multi-company run as a co-organizer, signed jointly by the organizing companies.

**When:** T-20w (once at least some projects have committed). Partner confirmations by T-14w.
**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer, co-signed with the co-organizer company's lead
**Audience:** OSPO lead or open source program contact at a prospective partner company

## Template

Version A: warm contact (you have met or discussed the model before)

```text
Subject: Sustaining Open Source Series with [Foundation]: would [Partner company] join us?

Hi [Contact first name],

We have news we wanted to share with you directly. The Sustaining Open Source Series with [Foundation] is happening, and [Project list] have committed to a multi-project cohort starting [Start date].

[Company] and [Co-organizer company] are co-organizing, and we would love to explore whether [Partner company] wants to join as a company collaborator. You know the model, but a lot has evolved since we last talked, and the multi-project, multi-company format is new.

Would you be open to a short conversation before [Confirm-by date] to go through the details? We have attached a one-page overview.

We would love to have [Partner company] at the table.

[Program organizer name] and [Co-organizer name]
```

Version B: new contact

```text
Subject: An invitation to join the Sustaining Open Source Series with [Foundation]

Hi [Contact first name],

I am reaching out because I think this could be a great fit for [Partner company].

This [season], [Company] and [Co-organizer company] are co-organizing the Sustaining Open Source Series with [Foundation]: a structured 10-week program where engineers from several companies volunteer their time to contribute to open source projects with guidance from project maintainers. It is a hands-on way for companies to invest in the health of the open source they depend on.

This run spans [N] projects: [Project list]. [One sentence on why this stack matters to the partner, e.g. "These sit underneath much of the data work in your industry." or "Given [Partner company]'s existing support for [Foundation], this could be a natural next step."]

What joining involves, in short:
- Encourage your engineers to apply and participate (about 2 to 4 hours a week for 10 weeks).
- Name a Team Lead and a few company mentors.
- Use your own internal approval and volunteer-hour process. [If relevant: Many partners match hours through their employee giving program.]

The organizing companies handle shared logistics: schedule, office hours, channels, and coordination with the projects.

Would you be open to a short conversation in the next couple of weeks? I have attached a one-page overview so you can see the details first.

Best,
[Program organizer name] and [Co-organizer name]
```

## How to use

- Pick Version A or B per recipient, and personalize the "why you" line. Every sent version in the source was tailored to the reader's existing relationship with the foundation or the organizers.
- Attach the Company Overview (03-company-overview.md).
- Confirm the project list is current on the day you send. Projects can drop out between commitment and launch.
- Send from one organizer with the other copied, so replies reach both.
- Track each contact and status in your outreach tracker; partner approvals can take several weeks.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public. Nothing here is specific to one company once names are placeholders.
- **Company-specific parts:** organizer and co-organizer names, project list, any mention of the partner's existing foundation relationship.
