# Project pitch email

Invites an open source project's maintainers and community to consider joining a Sustaining Open Source Series, in the open, on the project's own mailing list or forum.

**When:** About 5 months before the start (T-20w), after any foundation conversation. Run it past the project's governance group first (PMC, steering council, or core team), then post publicly.
**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer, ideally someone the project already knows (a contributor, foundation member, or company open source lead)
**Audience:** The project's maintainers and contributors, on the project's development list, forum, or discussion board

## Template

```text
Subject: [DISCUSS] A mentored contributor program for [Project] (starting [Start month and year])

Hi all,

TL;DR: We'd like to run a small, mentored contributor program for [Project] [and related projects] starting [Start month and year], and we're looking for projects and maintainers who'd like to take part. If you're interested, reply here [or: reply here to join a short call with the company organizers]. Questions, concerns, and "not right now" are all fine replies. Details below.

WHO WE ARE
I'm [Program organizer name], [role, and your connection to the project]. This is the Sustaining Open Source Series: companies ([Partner companies]) send engineers to contribute to open source projects for ten weeks, with the projects' own maintainers as mentors.

WHY [PROJECT]
[One or two honest sentences: why this project matters to the participating companies, and why now.]

WHAT WE'VE LEARNED SO FAR
[If you have run it before: We have run this model [N] times. Most recently, [cohort name] paired [N] engineers with [Previous project] maintainers for ten weeks, producing [N] contributions and [N] merged pull requests across [N] repositories. (Write-up: [Link])]
[Optional quote from a past maintainer, with their permission: "[Quote]," [Name], [role].]

WHAT WE'RE PROPOSING
- Projects: [Project list]
- Size: a deliberately small cohort, at most about [N] participants [from each company]. We'd rather keep it small and make sure every pull request gets reviewed.
- Timing: ten weeks, starting around [Start month and year].
- Companies: open to more. If your employer might want to send engineers, mentors, or both, please get in touch.

HOW IT WORKS
- Companies recruit and select participants. Applicants fill in an intake survey, then pick a real issue and explain how they'd approach it.
- [If participants contribute on their own time: Participants donate their own time.] [If participants contribute on work time: Participants contribute with their managers' approval.] Roughly 2 to 4 hours a week.
- Weekly office hours (one hour on video) start with a shared topic, such as testing, writing a good pull request, or the project's AI contribution policy, then split into project breakouts.
- Each company provides its own mentors for the basics: development setup, finding your way around the code, and pull request etiquette. That keeps beginner questions off your plate.
- The companies handle logistics: scheduling, channels, tracking progress, and non-ranked progress reporting. [If any participating company matches volunteer hours: Some companies also match volunteer hours with donations to [Foundation] through their giving programs.]
- Contributing means more than pull requests: participants join community meetings, help with triage and docs, review, and answer questions in your channels.

WHAT WE'D ASK OF EACH PROJECT
1. Curated work. Good first issues, plus a few larger pieces of work a small team could finish in ten weeks, and other ways to take part (meetings, triage, docs). This matters more than anything else on the list.
2. Project mentors. Ideally 3 to 4 maintainers or regular contributors, so there's cover when someone is away. About 2 hours a week each, and at least 3 office hours sessions during the series.
3. Timely review. Participants will open pull requests, and the program fails if they sit unreviewed. Tell us honestly how much review you can handle, and we'll size the cohort to match.
4. Somewhere to ask questions in the open, such as a channel in your existing chat.
5. Your contribution guidelines, including any policy on AI-assisted contributions.
6. A short retrospective at the end, so the next cohort goes better.

WHAT IT ISN'T
Participants follow the project's normal process, and your maintainers decide what gets merged. The program gives no company a say in roadmaps or governance. Each project sets its own list of work, and each project decides whether to join.

WHAT YOU GET
A small group of new contributors who are vetted, mentored, and committed for a set period, working on the things you choose. Onboarding is shared, so you aren't mentoring people one at a time. And a realistic chance that some of them stay once the ten weeks are over.

WHAT IT WOULD TAKE TO START IN [START MONTH]
- [Month, about 3 months before]: Projects confirm they're in and name their mentors.
- [Month, about 2 months before]: Projects curate their work lists while companies recruit and select participants.
- [Start month]: The ten weeks begin.

The goal isn't a burst of pull requests. It's people who learn how your community works and keep showing up after the ten weeks end.

If you're interested, reply here. I can also connect you with maintainers from past cohorts so you can hear honestly what it was like for them.

Thanks,
[Program organizer name]
```

## How to use

- **Ask the project's governance group first.** Share the draft with them before posting to the public list, and ask for their blessing (or "not now").
- **Proof points must be checkable.** Only include numbers you can source, with the cohort and period named, and link a public write-up. Never estimate. Get a maintainer's permission before quoting them.
- **Correct names.** Use each project's, foundation's, and company's own spelling and capitalization.
- **Pick one time line.** Keep either "donate their own time" or "with their managers' approval," based on how participating companies run it. If companies differ, say so.
- **Reporting.** Say "non-ranked progress reporting" unless all companies have agreed to a public dashboard. Leaderboards can be gamed and raise privacy concerns.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public. The structure works for any project and any set of companies; only the proof points and time policy change.
- **Company-specific parts:** proof points from past runs, whether participants use their own time or work time, matching programs, the list of participating companies.
