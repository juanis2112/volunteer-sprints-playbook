# Participant FAQ

Answers to the questions prospective and accepted participants ask most, published alongside the recruitment announcement.

**When:** Publish at T-10w with the announcement; update after the info session (T-6w) and again before kickoff.

**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer.

**Audience:** Prospective and accepted participants (and their managers).

## Template

### About the series

**What is the Sustaining Open Source Series?**
A 10-week program where employees contribute to [Project list] with mentorship from project maintainers and experienced Company mentors. It runs from [Start date] to [End date] and is organized by [Company] with [Foundation] and [Partner companies].

**Who is organizing it?**
- [Foundation], the nonprofit home of [Project list]
- Project mentors from each participating project
- [Company] and [Partner companies], each contributing Company mentors and participants
- [Company-specific: the internal teams backing the series at your company, e.g. your open source program office]

**Who should apply?**
Everyone with [Language or skill requirement] is welcome, regardless of region or experience level. First-time contributors get a supported, structured path in. Experienced contributors get direct access to maintainers, dedicated time for meaningful problems, and a chance to build their standing in the community.

**Why participate?**
- Project impact: contribute to tools used around the world.
- Personal impact: grow your skills, expand your network across companies, and join a welcoming community.
- Public good: help sustain the open source infrastructure everyone relies on.

### Time and logistics

**How much time does it take?**
Plan on about [N] hours a week for 10 weeks, plus a short weekly check-in with your mini-cohort lead. Work is asynchronous and flexible. Most people find the time is front-loaded in the first weeks while they set up their environment.

**What are the dates?**
- Applications close: [Application deadline]
- Decisions sent: [Decision date]
- Confirm your spot by: [Confirm-by date]
- Series runs: [Start date] to [End date]

**What happens over the 10 weeks?**
- Week 0 (before kickoff): join the community hub, set up your development environment for both projects, complete any internal steps.
- Week 1: onboarding, repository tours, finish setup.
- Weeks 2-3: find a starting point, such as a good first issue, a docs fix, or a test.
- Weeks 4-9: project work, share-outs, theme sessions with mentors, mid-series survey.
- Week 10: wrap up open work, thank your collaborators, share feedback, plan how you'll keep contributing.

**When are office hours?**
Every [Office hours day and time, with UTC] on [Zoom link]. Office hours are a time to ask questions, pair, share progress or blockers, and hear from maintainers. Please attend at least [N] sessions; more is better. [If you run a regional cohort: A separate session for [Region] runs at [Regional office hours time, with UTC].]

**How do I talk with other participants and mentors?**
Join the community hub ([Hub workspace name]) with your personal email, then ask a Team Lead to add you to [Hub channel]. You'll also join each project's own community channels for your primary and secondary projects.

### Mentors and support

**Who are the mentors?**
- Project mentors: maintainers and core contributors from each project, who know the code and the community best.
- Company mentors: experienced contributors from [Company] and [Partner companies] who help with setup, finding issues, and navigating the community.
- Mini-cohort leads: each participant is placed in a small group with a named lead who checks in one-on-one each week.

**What do mentors do?**
Answer questions in project channels, attend office hours, help you find right-sized work, review or route your pull requests, and share their own open source journeys.

**How do I get eyes on my work?**
When you open a pull request or comment on an issue, follow the project's contributing guide. It is usually fine to tag a series mentor; if not, they will route it to the right reviewer.

### Contributing

**What counts as contributing?**
Much more than pull requests. All of these count:
- Learning the project and setting up your environment
- Joining the project's chat and answering questions
- Attending community or working group meetings
- Triaging issues and reproducing bugs
- Reviewing pull requests
- Improving documentation, examples, and tests
- Writing code

Success in the series means learning how to engage with a community and continuing to show up, not a pull request count.

**How do I find things to work on?**
We'll work on this together in office hours and in [Hub channel]. Finding the right-sized issue is an art. To get a head start, read each project's contributing guide and search for labels like "good first issue."

**Which GitHub account do I use?**
Your public github.com account. All series work happens in the open. [Company-specific: follow your company's guidance on linking a work email or attribution, e.g. "Add your work email as a secondary address on your GitHub profile. Do not make it your primary address."]

**Can I use AI tools?**
Follow each project's AI policy first; many projects require disclosure, and some reject undisclosed AI-generated contributions. Our principles:
- Disclose AI assistance when the project asks, and when in doubt.
- Understand and be able to explain every line you submit.
- Don't use AI to write your comments or replies to maintainers on your behalf.
- Respect reviewer time: a small, well-understood change beats a large generated one.
[Company-specific: your company's policy on AI tools for open source contribution.]
See [Collaboration guidance link] for details.

### Hours, approval, and selection

**Do my hours count for anything?**
[If your company has a volunteer-hour matching program: Yes. Series time counts as volunteer time and is eligible for matching through [Matching program name]. Track your time from when you start learning a project, including reading, setup, discussion, and reviews. Log it through [Hour tracking process] by [Date].]
[If your company does not have a matching program: Delete this question, or explain how your company recognizes the work.]

**Do I need my manager's approval?**
[If your company requires internal approval to contribute: Yes. If selected, file [Approval process] by [Date]. Your manager will be asked to confirm. We also send managers a short note explaining the program.]
[If not required: We still encourage you to tell your manager you're applying.]

**How does selection work?**
A small team of reviewers from the organizing team reads every application. We look for real use of the projects and a specific idea of what you want to work on, open source experience or genuine excitement, effort in the written answers, and the time to commit. We also balance the cohort across projects based on mentor capacity, so placement depends on both your ranking and available mentors. Prior series participation neither helps nor hurts. Everyone hears back by [Decision date], and people on the waitlist hear by [Waitlist decision date].

**What if I'm not selected?**
There are other ways to contribute. [If your company has a general open source contribution program: You can contribute on your own through [Company program].] We expect to run future cohorts and hope you'll apply again.

**What if I can't continue during the series?**
Tell your mini-cohort lead or the Program organizer as early as possible. It's completely fine, and it helps us offer the spot to someone on the waitlist.

**What's the long-term hope?**
That participants become ongoing contributors to these projects, and eventually mentors in future cohorts.
