# Open Source Volunteer Sprint: Organizer Playbook

**Authors:** Juanita Gomez  
**Last updated:** October 2026

A recurring skills-based volunteering sprint where selected employees contribute to open source projects over a 10-week window with hands-on mentorship from project maintainers. This guide is the primary operational reference for the program organizer. It covers everything from recruiting mentors three months out to archiving materials after the sprint closes.

## Roles

### Program organizer

Owns timeline, communications, selection, documentation, calendar setup, mentor coordination, and weekly operating rhythm.

### Mentors

Help participants get started, support environment setup, guide issue selection, attend office hours, monitor Slack, and participate in post-mortem activities. Expect roughly 2 hours per week.

### Volunteers

Join Slack, set up their environment, attend kickoff and office hours, work from the project backlog, and communicate early if they cannot continue.

## Stage 1: 3 months before sprint

The goal at this stage is to confirm which projects and mentors are in, secure the necessary partnership agreements, and decide what documentation needs to exist before outreach starts.

### Task 1: Recruit mentors & scope projects

Start from the launch checklist and planning notes. Create a single source-of-truth table with one row per project — recording maintainer contact, internal mentor contact, Slack channel, issue backlog location, and expected contribution types. Confirm the sprint window, which projects are participating, which external partners are joining, the internal owner for each project, and whether the sprint includes non-code contributions.

Confirm mentor commitments for each project. Require a backup mentor so office hours are never uncovered, and record where volunteers will ask questions if the primary mentor is unavailable. Do not proceed to outreach until every project has a named mentor and owner.

### Task 2: Legal & partnerships

Confirm partnership agreements and any grant paperwork are in place before outreach starts. Confirm whether NDAs or other collaboration agreements are required for external partners. If external guests need access to your company's internal systems or collaboration tools, follow your company's external collaborator onboarding process. Keep a simple tracker of what is signed, what is pending, and who is accountable.

### Task 3: Plan documentation

Decide which documents must exist before outreach starts, where they will live (internal wiki vs. Google Docs vs. Slack), and what a new volunteer must read first. Keep one canonical version of each document.

Minimum required set:
* organizer guide (this document)
* volunteer guide
* mentor guide
* office hours agenda template
* selection rubric

Create a document inventory with owners and URLs before proceeding to Stage 2.

### Task 4: Define success metrics

Agree on what success looks like for this cycle before outreach starts. Lock the metrics early so reporting at the end is straightforward and not reverse-engineered from whatever data happens to be available.

**Participation metrics**
* Number of applications received
* Number of volunteers selected and accepted
* Volunteer retention rate — percentage who remained active through the final week
* Number of projects covered (and whether all projects had active volunteers)

**Contribution metrics**
* PRs opened by sprint volunteers
* PRs merged by sprint volunteers
* Issues closed or commented on
* Non-code contributions completed (docs, triage, testing — if applicable)

**Experience metrics**
* Mid-cohort survey completion rate and average satisfaction score
* End-of-series survey completion rate and average satisfaction score
* Mentor retro: whether mentors would participate again (yes / with changes / no)

**Program health metrics**
* Percentage of office hours sessions with full mentor coverage
* Number of volunteers who needed waitlist replacement mid-sprint
* Time from form close to selection communications sent

Set a target for each metric or at minimum a "good / acceptable / needs improvement" band. Record actuals in the sprint tracker at the end of Stage 6. Update this list before the next cycle if a metric turned out not to be useful.

## Stage 2: 2 months before sprint

This stage covers all outreach, documentation preparation, and platform setup. The goal is to have everything in place — form live, docs ready, platforms set up — well before selection begins.

### Task 1: Launch outreach

#### 1. Create the volunteer interest form

Create or update the interest form for this cycle. Check that the title, description, questions, and confirmation message match the sprint you are launching. Include the volunteer deadline in the description and on the final screen. Test the form once yourself before distribution. Confirm the linked response spreadsheet is correct and accessible. The form should be open for at least a month.

**Interest form template**

The sections and questions below represent a recommended form structure. Adapt as needed for each cycle — update project names, dates, and mentor names in the description before publishing.

*Form header:*

> About the Program: [Sprint name] — [Your company's team], in collaboration with [partners], is organizing a skills-based volunteering sprint to support [project list]. Over 10 weeks, volunteers receive hands-on mentorship from project maintainers and attend weekly office hours. Volunteer hours are eligible for matching through [your company's volunteer hour matching program].
>
> Sprint dates: [start date] – [end date]
>
> Deadline: [application deadline]
>
> Announcement: [notification date]

**Section 1: Who Are You?**
* Full Name \*
* Location \*
* Team \*
* GitHub Handle (if any)
* Email Address \*

**Section 2: Your Python Skills**
* How would you rate your Python skills? \* (scale 1–10, Beginner to Expert)
* How comfortable are you with scientific data crunching — dataframes, arrays, numbers? \* (scale 1–10, Beginner to Expert)

**Section 3: Rank Your Projects**
* Familiarity & prior contribution \* (1 = never touched it, 5 = I've contributed before) — one row per project
* Excitement to contribute here! \* (1 = curious to learn more, 5 = I can't wait to dig in) — one row per project

**Section 4: Your Open Source Story**
* Have you contributed to open source before? \* (Yes / No)
* If yes — tell us about it, including if you've contributed to any of the projects above.
* What's pulling you toward contributing to these projects?

**Section 5: Can You Commit?**
* Please check all that apply: \*
  * I can carve out ~2–4 hours/week for the 10-week series
  * I can attend at least 2 office hours ([day and time])
* What do you hope to walk away with from this experience?
* Anything else we should know about you?

#### 2. Host at least one information session and Q&A

While the form is open, host at least one information session. The program lead presents highlights from the previous cycle, outlines goals for the upcoming sprint, and opens for Q&A. Include the session date and link in all distribution communications so potential volunteers can attend before deciding to apply.

Aim for two sessions: one about a week after the form opens and a second about two weeks after. This gives late responders a chance to attend before the deadline.

#### 3. Draft internal advertisement and comms

##### Slack & email message

Use the approved message below as the starting point. Keep the first sentence clear and practical. Include program dates, supported projects, and the matching-hours note. End with an explicit deadline. Proofread all links before posting. Make a shorter variant if a channel has strict posting norms, and include both calls to action in both versions.

> Before using: update the partner names, project list, sprint dates, and application deadline.

```text
[Your company's team], in collaboration with [partners], is organizing a skills-based volunteering sprint to support several open source projects: [project list].

The program runs for 10 weeks, from [start date] to [end date], with hands-on mentorship from project maintainers and weekly office hours. Participants will work alongside maintainers and contributors from [company list]. Volunteer hours are eligible for matching through [your company's volunteer hour matching program].

We will also host information sessions on [dates] where you can learn more and ask questions — details will follow.

We are looking for:

1. Volunteers — No previous experience with these projects is required. If you are interested in potentially volunteering, please express your interest here: [form link]
2. Mentors — We are looking for people familiar with one or more of these projects who can help volunteers get started and contribute effectively. If you are interested, please reach out on Slack or by email at [organizer email].

Deadline: [application deadline]

Join our mailing list to stay up to date on this and future open source initiatives: [mailing list link]

Please share with colleagues who may be interested. Feel free to contact me if you have any questions.
```

#### 4. Send Slack outreach to relevant channels

Find the channels most relevant to the projects in the current sprint. Use a tracker with columns for channel name, URL, date posted, reminder date, and notes. Get approval from a channel owner or moderator before posting if the channel has one.

During launch week, check form responses daily, answer Slack questions, update the outreach tracker, and follow up on missing owners.

**Project-specific channels** (target channels where practitioners of the relevant technologies are active — for Python-adjacent projects, look for channels focused on Python, scientific computing, data science, and related libraries):

* Identify channels where people working on or with the sprint's target projects are likely to be active
* Include any working groups, guilds, or communities of practice relevant to the technology area

**General channels** (broader reach, include for any sprint):

* Engineering community channels
* Early-career channels (interns, new grads)
* Open source or developer community channels
* Relevant department or team announcement channels

#### 5. Send email outreach

The primary list for email outreach is the mailing list of people who have previously expressed interest in the program. Make sure the list is up to date before sending.

Open the email with a line explaining why they're receiving it — for example: "You're receiving this because you've previously expressed interest in our open source volunteer sprints."

Use the same message template from sub-task 3. The subject line should make the volunteer and open source angle clear at a glance. Send to all addresses at once rather than segmenting by category. If you update or create a distribution list, document its name and owner in the sprint tracker.

#### 6. Contact internal newsletters and announcement channels

Your company's internal newsletters and announcement channels are among the most visible channels for this type of program. Reach out to the relevant owners with a short pitch and the announcement copy.

**Who to contact** (identify the equivalent at your company):
* Company-wide news or internal comms team
* Engineering or developer newsletter
* Manager newsletter or leadership update
* Learning & development newsletter
* Any digital signage or intranet announcement channels

Use the announcement below as a starting template. Update all sprint-specific details — project names, dates, mentors, and links — before submitting.

> Before using: update the project name, sprint dates, mentor names, and all links.

```text
Contribute your skills to the popular open source project — [project name].

In collaboration with [partners] and the [project] maintainers, [your company] is kicking off a 10-week skills-based volunteer opportunity in mid-[month].

Among the goals is to help employees contribute to [project], [brief project description].

In addition to building valuable skills for employees, volunteer hours are eligible for matching through [your company's volunteer hour matching program].

No Prior [project] Knowledge Needed

The project kicks off [date] and runs through [end date].

Mentors from [company list] and [project] will be on hand via Slack and during regular weekly office hours to help guide and answer questions.

The only requirement for volunteering is familiarity with Python programming. Knowledge of [project] is not a prerequisite.

Why Join?

Goals for the project include:

- Encouraging more employees to contribute to an open-source project that's important to our work.
- Influencing through contributions. Open-source projects like [project] evolve through community input. The more employees contribute, the greater our ability to shape critical features.
- Fostering community connections. This program gets engineers started with mentoring and offers opportunities to work with volunteers across organizations.
- Upskilling. The program provides opportunities for volunteers to improve their programming abilities.
- Building knowledge. [Project description and relevance.]
- Championing [foundation], a nonprofit that supports [project] and promotes accessible, reproducible scientific and technical computing.
- Volunteer-time matching. Participants can log their hours and get a match through [your company's matching program].

Those interested in volunteering can talk to their managers and fill out this form to sign up.
```

#### 7. Other outreach opportunities

Consider pitching a tech talk through an internal engineering community or similar internal group. A short session with a project maintainer — covering what the project is, what kinds of contributions are needed, and what participants can expect — significantly increases awareness and interest. Identify the relevant internal community organizers at your company and reach out to propose a session.

### Task 2: Prepare documentation

#### 8. Prepare volunteer and mentor documentation

Build two separate reading paths — one for volunteers and one for mentors. Create an internal FAQ section so repeated organizer answers live in one place. Include or link the Series Roles & Expectations document in the mentor path. Include or link the Collaboration Guidance document for the internal team, especially for Zoom, Slack, and AI usage norms. Keep instructions concrete: where to join, what to read, what to do in week 1, and how to ask for help.

#### 9. Build the training and onboarding support set

Identify relevant internal learning courses and add them to the volunteer onboarding path with one sentence each explaining who should take them.

Add a non-code contributions section listing acceptable contribution types — documentation, triage, testing, issue reproduction, community support, release/process work — confirming first which types each participating project allows. Add a short open source primer covering expected etiquette, issue-first workflow, and working with maintainers. Add a note on whether any internal AI tools can support organizers or volunteers and under what boundaries.

### Task 3: Set up platforms

#### 10. Set up the volunteer hour tracking page

Your company likely has a volunteer hour tracking or matching platform (such as Benevity/Inspire 365, YourCause, or similar). Create a page for this sprint cycle so participants can log their hours and receive any matching benefit.

When setting up the page, cover the following:

**Title** — Use the sprint name in a way that is immediately recognizable to employees. Example: NumFOCUS Volunteer Sprint Fall 2026. Reuse the naming pattern from any prior cycles for consistency.

**Summary** — Write a short summary explaining that this is a 10-week volunteer sprint supporting open source projects and that employees will self-track their hours.

**Full description** — Include the sprint dates, project list, a note that participation is selected rather than open signup, instructions for logging volunteer hours, and where to find the volunteer guide and contact person.

**Category** — Choose whichever category your platform offers for business or skills-based volunteering initiatives, as distinct from standard local volunteer events.

**When and where** — Set it up as virtual. Use the overall sprint window rather than individual shifts for every contribution block.

**Contact information** — Use the organizer contact that appears in communications.

**Cause / organization** — Link to the relevant foundation (e.g., NumFOCUS). Verify the correct cause before publishing.

**Shifts** — Use a simple start-to-finish setup rather than multiple rotating shifts.

**Image** — Optional. If used, match open source sprint branding.

**Visibility** — Use by invitation only. This is a cohort-based sprint with controlled participation. Company-wide visibility would make it searchable by all employees, which is not the intent.

**Volunteer limit** — Set the maximum based on the final cohort size.

**Waitlist** — Enable if you want overflow interest captured in the page. Otherwise manage it through the separate interest form.

Once all fields are complete, submit and capture the generated link. Use that link in volunteer acceptance emails, onboarding materials, and time-tracking instructions. Save the final link in the sprint tracker.

Tell participants they can log time as they go or track it offline and submit later. Remind them that all volunteer time must be submitted within the calendar year. Plan reminders during the sprint and again near the end.

Include time-tracking instructions in the acceptance email, the volunteer guide, and the end-of-sprint reminder. Suggested page description copy:

```text
This volunteer opportunity supports an open source volunteer sprint program in collaboration with external partners and maintainers. Participants contribute over the course of the sprint window and are responsible for tracking their own volunteer hours. This is a cohort-based opportunity for selected participants rather than a general drop-in event. If you have questions about participation or time tracking, contact the program organizer.
```

#### 11. Set up the Slack workspace structure

Create or confirm the sprint's Slack channel structure before outreach goes out. Required channels: a mentor coordination channel, a participant announcement channel, and one channel per project. Track which external or partner team members still need workspace access and follow up until they have all joined.

## Stage 3: 1 month before sprint

At this stage, the survey has closed and the focus shifts entirely to volunteer selection. Complete selection before the week-before-sprint onboarding begins.

### Task 1: Run volunteer selection

**1. Finalize volunteer selection criteria.** Start from the selection criteria document in this playbook folder and adapt it if needed. Confirm who participates in the selection meeting and block that time on the calendar before reviewing applications. Suggested criteria: motivation and clarity of interest, availability during the sprint window, alignment with supported projects, mentor capacity for the chosen project, and whether the applicant is likely to follow through.

**2. Run selection and document decisions.** Before the meeting, clean the form responses and group candidates by likely project interest. During the meeting, use one live tracker so decisions are captured as you go. Mark each person as accepted, waitlisted, declined, or needs follow-up. If mentor capacity is the limiting factor, document that explicitly.

**3. Draft and send selection communications.** Start from the email templates in this playbook folder — it contains acceptance, waitlist, and reminder email drafts ready to customize. The acceptance email must include: selection confirmation, sprint dates, reading list, Slack join instructions, intro session details, office hours details, and a commitment confirmation request. Explicitly ask accepted volunteers to notify the organizers if they cannot commit so someone from the waitlist can be invited.

## Stage 4: 1 week before sprint

This stage is about onboarding everyone and making sure everything is set up for day 1.

### Task 1: Onboard mentors

**4. Curate good first issues.** Request a starter backlog appropriate for first-time contributors for each project. Assign one person per project to keep the issue list current during the sprint. Confirm with each mentor that their project's good first issues are labeled, unblocked, and have enough context for a new contributor to get started without prior knowledge of the codebase. This is a mentor-owned task — confirm delivery before the kickoff meeting.

**5. Host the mentor kickoff meeting.** Bring all mentors together before the sprint starts to align on expectations, cover logistics, and answer questions. Confirm each mentor knows the escalation path for volunteer blockers and has reviewed the good first issues for their project.

**6. Finish mentor onboarding.** Send mentors a focused message with required actions — not a long generic note. They need to: join the Slack workspace and required channels, confirm availability for the sprint window, read the mentor expectations document, and attend kickoff if possible. Verify access rather than assuming it. Send office hours calendar invites.

Before kickoff, confirm each mentor has: joined the required Slack workspace and channels, read mentor expectations, reviewed good first issues, confirmed office hours availability, and knows the escalation path for blockers.

### Task 2: Onboard volunteers

**7. Set up intro sessions and calendar holds.** Use the info sessions agenda template in this playbook folder as the starting point. Write a tentative schedule before sending invites. Block calendars for key stakeholders. Invite mentors as optional attendees — attendance is strongly encouraged because it helps them connect with volunteers early. Include the purpose of the session in the calendar description: onboarding, expectations, communication norms, and first steps. Finalize who is speaking, the order of topics, and whether the session will be recorded. If there are multiple time zones, pick one default session and offer a backup if needed.

**8. Finish volunteer onboarding.** Send volunteers the guide and tell them which sections are mandatory before day 1. Make joining Slack a required first step. Explicitly confirm each participant has been added to the calendar for all sprint sessions. Track completion so follow-up is easy.

Before kickoff, confirm each volunteer has: accepted the participation offer, joined Slack, received calendar invites, read starter documentation, begun environment setup, and knows who to contact for help.

### Task 3: Set up office hours

Confirm and finalize the recurring office hours time — pick a slot that works across the time zones of your participants and mentors. Set up one stable Zoom link and decide in advance whether recording is enabled and where links will be saved. Draft the running agenda template before the first session so kickoff starts from a solid foundation. Confirm mentor coverage across all projects before kickoff.

## Stage 5: During the sprint

This stage covers the weekly operating rhythm once the sprint is live.

Weekly habit: confirm office hours coverage, publish or refresh the agenda, post wins in Slack, review the activity tracker, follow up on blockers, and update the documentation with anything that came up more than once.

### Task 1: Kickoff & week 1

Publish the week 1 agenda at least one business day before the kickoff session. Include who to contact for project questions, logistics questions, and blockers. Make sure volunteers know where weekly updates will be posted and introduce communication norms at kickoff.

### Task 2: Weekly operations

Use the agenda template drafted in Stage 4. Reuse it each week and keep it running rather than recreating it from scratch. After each session, note decisions, blockers, and follow-ups. Confirm mentor coverage before every session.

Post a weekly wins message with shout-outs, merged PRs, first issues completed, or learning milestones. Use a light-touch follow-up for inactive volunteers before assuming they dropped. If someone cannot continue, move quickly to replace them from the waitlist if the timing still makes sense.

Use one weekly tracker for PRs, issues, comments, and notable milestones. If a project has too many blocked volunteers, escalate quickly and rebalance work. Record recurring onboarding pain points so they can be fixed in this guide for the next cycle.

### Task 3: Feedback loops

Run the mid-cohort survey around week 5. Keep it short and action-oriented. Ask mentors what volunteers were blocked on most often.

At sprint close, run the end-of-series survey and hold a mentor retro. Capture what should change in documentation, issue curation, onboarding, or communications. Add a short section for "how to keep contributing after the sprint."

## Stage 6: After the sprint

Complete this stage within two weeks of the sprint close date.

### Task 1: Review outcomes

Pull together the mid-cohort and end-of-series survey results. If the mentor retro has not happened yet, schedule it within the first week after the close date. Summarize key metrics: number of participants, PRs opened, PRs merged, issues closed. Document what volunteers and mentors said was most and least useful. Write findings directly into the relevant sections of this guide so future cycles benefit.

### Task 2: Archive & close

Duplicate the cycle tracker tab before clearing it so the record is preserved. Create a named subfolder for the cycle (e.g., `Fall_2026`) within the playbook folder and move cycle-specific files there. Save office hours recordings and notes to the shared location. Store the final volunteer guide, mentor guide, and all communications in that folder.

Write a short outcomes summary — one page or less — covering participation numbers, project contributions, and volunteer experience highlights. Share it with stakeholders who supported the program.

Then return to this guide and update whatever was hard, wrong, or missing. Update the Open items section below and capture new outreach lessons in Stage 2, Task 1. The next organizer depends on this step.
