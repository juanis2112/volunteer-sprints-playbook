# Foundation pitch email

Proposes a Sustaining Open Source Series to a foundation or project so maintainers can decide whether to explore it.

**When:** T-24w (first contact). Start any agreement by T-16w.
**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer
**Audience:** Foundation staff (program or community lead) who can forward to the maintainers who own contributor strategy

## Template

```text
Subject: Partnership idea: a Sustaining Open Source Series for [Project]

Hi [Foundation contact first name],

Following up on our conversation, I wanted to share a short summary of a contributor program we would like to explore with [Project].

We think it can be a high-value, low-overhead way for the [Project] community to gain contribution capacity and meet motivated people who may stay involved long term.

The model
The Sustaining Open Source Series is a 10-week cohort program. Employees of [Company] [and [Partner companies]] contribute to an open source project with guidance from company mentors and project maintainers. [If you have run it before: We have run this model [N] times, most recently with [Previous project]. Over [N] weeks, [N] participants contributed to [N] issues and many are still engaged with the project.] The goal is to turn users into sustained, responsible open source contributors and, over time, community members who keep showing up.

What it would look like for [Project]
1. Focused contribution. Participants work on issues and areas the maintainers choose. Maintainers set the direction; we staff the work.
2. Mentorship that lowers maintainer load. [Company] provides its own technical mentors who work alongside project mentors, so participants are well supported before they reach a reviewer.
3. Training and etiquette. We host weekly office hours covering the codebase, testing, debugging, review etiquette, and the project's AI policy, so participants show up prepared.
4. More than code. Participants are encouraged to join community meetings, help with triage, improve docs, and answer questions in project channels, not only open pull requests.
5. A community channel. Participants connect with maintainers in a channel the project chooses.
[If your company has a volunteer-hour matching program: Volunteer hours are eligible for matching through [Matching program name], with donations going to [Foundation] on behalf of the project. There is no fee for the partnership itself.]

What we would ask of the project
- A short project profile: onboarding docs, a set of series-ready issues or contribution areas, and reviewer availability.
- [N] maintainers who can join some of the weekly office hours over 10 weeks.

What it isn't: participants follow the project's normal process, maintainers decide what gets merged, and no company gets a say in roadmaps or governance.

[Project] matters a great deal to [Company], and we want to create a space where our employees contribute authentically and become strong participants in this community.

Would you be able to share this with the maintainers who look after contributor strategy, and let us know if they are interested in an exploratory conversation? Questions, concerns, and "not right now" are all fine answers.

Thank you,
[Program organizer name]
[Title], [Company]
```

## How to use

- Only include the track-record line if you have verified numbers from a prior run, with period and source. Otherwise delete it; never estimate.
- Delete the matching paragraph if your company has no matching program. If you keep it, be precise that money flows only through the hours match.
- Name the specific reasons the project matters to your company. Generic praise reads as a template.
- Pair with the project pitch email (02) when you're ready to go to the project's community directly.
- Keep the ask small at this stage. Details (profile, issue counts, dates) come in the kickoff and project profile request.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public core + company add-on. The pitch structure is universal; track record and matching are company-specific.
- **Company-specific parts:** prior-run results, matching program name and mechanics, any agreement or name-use language your legal team requires, signature.
