# Acceptance email

Tells a selected applicant they're in, gives their project placement and key dates, and lists what to do before kickoff.

**When:** T-5w (as soon as selection is final), with [Confirm-by date] about one week later (T-4w).

**Sent by / owner:** Program organizer (individually or via mail merge), with the mini-cohort lead copied.

**Audience:** Each accepted participant.

## Template

```text
Subject: 🎉 You're in! Welcome to the Sustaining Open Source Series

Hi [Participant first name]!

Congratulations! You've been selected for the Sustaining Open Source Series. You'll be contributing to [Primary project] as your primary project, with [Secondary project] as your secondary.

Please confirm by [Confirm-by date] with a quick "I'm in!" reply.

If you can't commit after all, please tell us as early as you can. It's completely fine, and it lets us offer your spot to someone on the waitlist.

Meet your mini-cohort lead
[Team Lead name] (copied here) will be your mini-cohort lead. They'll check in with you one-on-one each week, help you get unstuck, and connect you with the right mentors. Say hi!

Key dates
- Office hours: [Office hours day and time, with UTC], [Start date] to [End date]. First session: [Start date].
- Zoom: [Zoom link]
- Please block the time on your calendar now. [If calendar invites are sent automatically: We'll also send a calendar invite.]

Your checklist before [Start date]
1. Join the community hub
   - Join [Hub workspace name] with your personal email: [Hub invite link]
   - Then message [Team Lead name] or [Program organizer name] so we can add you to [Hub channel], where you'll meet participants and mentors from [Partner companies] and the projects.
   - Introduce yourself and say what you'd like to work on (code, docs, testing, triage, reviews).
2. Set up your development environment for both projects
   - Setup guide: [Setup guide link]
   - Project guide (overview, mentors, and channels for each project): [Project guide link]
   - Join each project's community: [Primary project]: [Project channel]. [Secondary project]: [Project channel].
   - Read each project's contributing guide; that's where you'll find good first issues.
3. Send us your public GitHub username if you didn't include one in your application.
4. Read the collaboration guidance (chat and video setup, community norms, AI use): [Collaboration guidance link]
[If your company requires internal approval to contribute: 5. File [Approval process] by [Date]. It routes to your manager for approval. We'll also send your manager a short note about the series.]
[If your company has a volunteer-hour matching program: 6. Track your hours through [Hour tracking process]. Volunteer hours are eligible for matching through [Matching program name]. Start counting from when you begin learning the project.]

Stay connected: [Company open source channel or newsletter]

We're excited to see what you'll build across [Primary project] and [Secondary project]!

Best,
[Program organizer name]
on behalf of the Sustaining Open Source Series team
```

## How to use

- Merge per person: first name, primary and secondary project, each project's channel, and the mini-cohort lead. Copy the lead on each email.
- Check every link, especially community invite links, which often expire; prefer non-expiring invites.
- Delete the conditional checklist items that don't apply and renumber.
- Track confirmations as they arrive; after [Confirm-by date], follow up once, then offer unconfirmed spots to the waitlist.
- If you'll be away during the confirmation window, name a backup (a Team Lead) in the email and ask people to reply-all.

## Notes for alignment

- **Recommendation:** Public core + company add-on. The structure and tone are shared; approval, hour tracking, and hub logistics vary.
- **Company-specific parts:** approval process, hour tracking and matching, open source channel or newsletter, whether calendar invites are automatic, guidance on personal vs work email for the hub.
